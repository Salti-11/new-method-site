70.3 World Championships – Lahti, Finland

By wakkatoo (Chris Chatham) · Race report · Sep 2023

What a weekend, what a privilege and what an honour.

To be in a position to not only accept, but then travel and compete in an event more the 15 THOUSAND km’s from home is not something i take lightly and I am eternally grateful for the support and encouragement I’ve gotten from my inner circle to do this event.

Race day itself was promising to be a good one, weather-wise. There was a wonderful

Sunrise when we stepped off the bus and the skies looked clear, despite the forecast for rain.

I hadn’t been feeling too well and was dealing with a pesky virus that popped its head up late on Friday night. On Saturday watching the women, I was at my worst to the point where I fell asleep on a park bench. Lots of fluid, Nurofen and rest and when I awoke on Sunday morning I felt the worst had passed, but I was still definitely affected. These are some of the variables that get thrown your way so rather than letting it affect me, it simply meant a slight alteration in my own expectations.

After a short amount of time adding nutrition to my bike and air into the tyres, Em and I headed down to race start. It was a very relaxed affair as I had heaps of time, but I must admit to a higher level of nerves than usual for a race if this distance. Personally, I’d placed a lot of importance on this race and the reasons I was doing it, so that was most likely the reason. Not forgetting the imposter syndrome that has been ever-present since the day I qualified, but I was coming to terms with that and figured many others were feeling the same way, so it’s probably natural.

After a warm-up run and some dry land swim exercises, it was time to get suited up and get in the zone for racing. I’d spoken to another GPC athlete who had raced the day before and she had given me the tip to get into swim marshal area early as they liked to get to into position well before start time. So it was a quick ‘see ya later’ to Em and into the chute I went. It was a bit of a wait, but I was calm and feeling ready. It was a bit of a different start with a jump in off a pier and at race briefing we were being told we had a choice of either jumping in feet first or diving in. For me, I was adamant I was going to dive and as we got closer to getting in the water (10 of us would start every 15secs) I got a bit more of a look at it. It seemed a few people were losing their goggles so a last minute change of mind had me going on feet first and it proved a correct choice – as I came up and looked to my right there were a couple of guys fixing goggles that had found their way around their necks.

It was a fresh water swim in a lake full of clean Nordic water. Temp was 18.6 degrees meaning it was wetsuit optional (so read:compulsory😉). It was a great temp – cool enough to warrant the wetsuit, but not so cold that the feet / face / hands go numb. It was a point to point swim with 3 turns. Swim bouys were numbered (a first for me) but I didn’t know how many there so that was more a point of interest for me rather than anything else😂. I felt really good in the water, I was happy with where I had positioned myself and found the occasional hip & toes to follow. From about the halfway point I was starting to notice a lot more athletes coming through.

The swim exit was a climb up some steps in front of a building called the Sibelius Hall. Apparently it’s world renowned, I’d never heard of it, but it’s impressive nonetheless. With the jump-in entry, followed by the climb out of the water, that’s the first swim where there has been an elevation loss and gain – not a usual occurrence.

T1 was a long one, with a run down to bag collection, into the change area and then an even longer distance back to the bike and out onto the course. Being in the first AG wave, the crowds were huge and the atmosphere at T1 was awesome.

The bike leg was something I’d been looking forward to – it was rolling hills and my lead up rides near where we were staying showed that the road conditions were really really good. It was a 1 lap ride and the first 35km was out of Lahti on a relatively flat part of the course. I was holding high 30’s into low 40’s in terms of speed and my power was being easily held around 240 watts (goal was around 210w). It was a lot of fun and I was feeling a lot more settled. The weather has started to turn and we’d had a few scattered showers. It was nothing too serious, just had to take a little bit of care on some corners (and the white paint on the zebra crossings) and the temp dropped a little bit causing the toes to go a little numb, but it wasn’t too much of a concern.

The next 45km was in gorgeous rolling hills heading back towards Lahti. The forests in Finland essentially have 3 types of trees – silver birch and 2 types of pine, but it is all so green and made for a very fun bike ride. The speed dropped a little due to the hills but the power was staying stable and I was in top of my nutrition so I was very happy with how I was going.

What I wasn’t happy with was the blatant cheating that was going on by others in my AG. Triathlon is a very individual sport and the rules are very clear when it comes to drafting. The race briefing was also very clear about the need to maintain a gap of 12m and they seemed very serious about enforcing it. Another rule is not crossing the centre line of the road – this is more to do with safety, but it was clear at race briefing that if you crossed the centre line you would be disqualified (and this was enforced on a leading male pro in somewhat controversial circumstances).

So to see in 3 separate occasions, very large groups of riders go through literally on each other wheel, sometimes 6 across the road (with 3 in the wrong lane) was very disappointing and you could see that on the faces of other riders who were doing the right thing. Each of these groups would have been easily 30-50 riders in size and whilst it was probably never going to impact on my final result, it was really disappointing to not only see it, but the lack of enforcement of the rules by the officials. There were 3 penalty tents and each time I expected to see a lot of riders doing their time, but in total I saw just 4 riders.

Anyway, enough of that – I raced honestly and can sleep at night knowing I did so at a world champs. There are a lot of guys that can’t say the same thing.

The final 10km into town and T2 was past the ski fields of Lahti and some nice punchy climbs. We’d had a little bit more rain and some poor bugger on a sharp left hander had come unstuck. He was getting medical treatment but when I rode past he was motionless…hope he was ok in the end.

Our AG definitely had the better weather for the bike which meant with about 500m to go I could really attack the final sharp climb. We had dubbed it ‘Water st’ in reference to a short sharp climb at home but the reality was it was a lot less steep that that. I missed seeing Em at this point as I was carrying good speed and the crowd was quite large and very vocal to my efforts….it was awesome!

It was then a sharp little decent into T2 which was another unique experience for me – it was indoors.

Similar to T1, T2 was a long run into an exhibition centre (think Jeff’s shed in Melbourne), self rack the bike then a run to bag collection then change area then out onto the course. Another tip is gotten from an athlete who’d run the day before was that not all of t2 was carpeted so it could be slippery, especially in cleats. I’ve never practiced dismounting the bike and leaving my shoes in the pedals so I wasn’t going to start now. So a little care was taken on the slippery surface. Once the bike was racked I then took off my shoes and carried them round to bag collection. That seemed to work well and it went without incident.

Onto the run and the crowds were huge. The rain had started to get heavier but that didn’t dampen their spirits although later conversations indicated it was taking a toll on those Age Groupers still out on the bike (which was nearly all of them).

It was a 2 lap course that started with a sharp flyover to really get the legs burning. What was really cool about it was that it was covered in all the flags of the competing nations….about 190 of them. We then entered the athletic arena for a lap in front of the grandstand crowd. This venue is quite spectacular as it is at the base of the giant ski jumps.

What followed next was a 5km uphill section that could only be described as a grind. Definitely steeper than a false flat, but not so steep you had to modify your run cadence / style. As a result, this was a sneaky hill- it really sapped the legs, which was a lot more noticeable on lap 2. From the top of this climb it was about 4km of really pretty down hill undulations through a residential section of Lahti before coming out beside the lake again. The locals were out, it was really scenic and the higher was…..I saw a squirrel! 😂

The final 2km of the lap was along the lake, past the iconic “Lahti” sign and then back to T2 where the crowds were getting really big. The vibe in this section was really incredible.

My intention for this race was to not embarrass myself, and I figured a time under 5hrs would be respectable. On the second lap I quickly knew that I would go under this time and knowing I still had Nice to go, I backed off a little and really focused on enjoying the run, thanking the volunteers and taking it all in. Did this affect my final placing? Yeah, probably…but so did those 3 large pelotons on the bike. I was here more to complete, rather than compete (to a point) and it was important to ensure I kept something in reserve for Nice.

The last 2km got a little tough. Not physically, just emotionally. I’ve been very motivated to get to this point in my fitness and I knew there were a lot of people back home following as well as watching live.

Crossing the line was amazing. It always is at these events, but this was on another level. I can only hope Nice can match it.

So Thankyou – to those that sent me a message, tagged me in finish’s line videos, helped out with my training and generally supported me in some way. It all really meant a lot.

A special thanks to my Coach, Stephane Vander Bruggen from Geelong Performance Coaching. I’ve been with GPC for about 5 years now and love the program and support Steph provides.

Thanks also to the wider Wakkatoo Crew. Most were sitting at home in front of a phone or computer watching, but they were definitely there with me in the run. My poor attempt at a heart on the finish line was for you guys. I’ll do better in Nice!

I also pointed 2 fingers at the sky – for 2 blokes who can’t be there, but I did it for them. All my events I do for them, because they can’t. Hope I did you boys proud.

And to Emmy. Very few words exist to express how much I love and appreciate the support she provides me on a daily basis. She is a rockstar….and me a poor student when it came to learning how to spectate. Love ya sweetie.

So now it’s off to Paris for a week to be a tourist then down to Nice to do the full distance World Champs. Nervous and excited are understatements, but bring it on – it’s a challenge I take head on.
