Full Distance Ironman World Championships – Nice, France

By wakkatoo (Chris Chatham) · Race report · Sep 2023

Wow. What an experience. Everything about the day, the course and how I went leaves me with a deep sense of satisfaction. It’s really hard to describe it any other way.

It was obvious this was an event on another level, security was like I’d never seen before with police manning the road closures, checkpoints to get in and out of the main race precinct and a flotilla of police and navy(?) boats protecting the swim course. I’m not sure what they were expecting, but whatever it was, they, were ready for it.

The course was incredible with us waking up to clear skies, the promise of another warm day and a race venue that was absolutely buzzing.

The impact of this heightened security was that Em and I got separated very early on. I went in to get my bike set up for the day (fan boy moment: spotted Jan Frodeno doing the same!) and then moved through to the “holding pen” before the race start. Normally I’d go for a run and have a swim but that simply wasn’t possible, there was no way out except via the swim start. Em was in a different area a bit closer to swim start, but spectators were very much separated. So a bit of warm up for me using the resistance bands and it was then about managing the nerves and awaiting my wave start.

Old school mate and fellow port-Mac qualifier Steve Watty is in the same age group as me and he spotted me in the wave start. A bit of a chat to do something other than be nervous, we also spotted our respective partners as we were about to enter the water.

Swim was a deep water start and not much time between each wave. I was in good spot and found my position (always outside left if I can). All week the water temps had been around the 24deg. so when we were informed at 5am that it had risen to 24.8deg. and was therefore a non-wetsuit swim (cutoff is 24.5) I wasn’t surprised, but needed to readjust my expectations. Wetsuit swims are always faster due to the buoyancy so I expected to go slower. I told Em anything under 1.15 would be great, but I quietly aimed for under 1.10.

There were still athletes in my AG still moving into the water when the gun went off, such was the size of our wave. The water was so clear, warm and this incredible shade of azure blue, it really was a lot of fun. The sun had risen but was hidden behind a rocky outcrop and after the intitial ‘out’ of 900m, turning back to the shore and getting a look at all the athletes behind really enforced in me that this event was on another level and living up to it’s world championship status early on. Getting back to shore meant the swim was halfway done, we had some turnaround bouys to get around before heading off on the second of our out and back ‘rectangles’. These turn bouys are always a bit of a pinch-point as athletes try to minimize the distance swum and you need to be prepared to cop an elbow or a knee or someone swimming over the top of you. Most likely you’ll do the same to someone else, it’s all part of the game.

I was in a good rhythm and felt I was swimming ok, and turning into the final return to shore I was feeling I was holding my own against the others wearing the same cap colour. Something I was expecting also was that the sun had gotten that little bit higher which meant for that entire swim back to shore, it was in your face and I was effectively swimming blind. It was nearly impossible to sight the swim bouys so I chased the feet and bubbles if other swimmers and hoped we were going the most efficient way back.

The beaches at Nice are not what we have in Australia. There is no sand, instead the beaches are made up of large loose rocks that are tricky to walk, let alone run, on. It also rises very quickly so you need to swim almost right to the waters edge. This created a bit of a log jam at swim exit but crossing the timing mat I was happy to see I swam a 1.08.00.

T1 was simple enough – grab my bag off the hook, get my bike gear on and nutrition in my pockets then head out to my bike, handing my bag back in with my swim gear. It felt a bit weird handing the bag back because it only had my goggles and swim caps – the weight of the wetsuit was missing and was strange.

The bike course was, in my opinion, truly epic. It was a single loop and extremely hard for spectators to get to so after I passed Em shortly after getting on the bike, she headed back to the hotel to have breakfast and a snooze, knowing there was nothing she could do on-course for the next 5-6hrs.

Well, that later became 6hrs19min, my slowest IM by quite a bit but also one that I think I executed my race plan the best and at times was able to use my long distance experience to make adjustments to that plan when circumstance dictated it.

It started with about 10km getting out of Nice and heading into the valley we were to initially climb out of and then descend back into. We’d been advised of a couple of ‘no-pass’ areas, with one at 5km because of the security measures that had been put in place. Despite knowing about it, I was surprised with how narrow they made the gap and it caught some others off guard with one guy ahead of me coming off his bike as a result.

Turning into the valley it wasn’t long before we were climbing. This wasn’t part of the main climb but they were done short, sharp and quite steep ‘punches’ that really got the legs awake.

I’d been really looking forward to this ride, but it was also very intimidating. I hadn’t been able to get out and do a proper ‘recce’ and there had been chatter ever since it was announced about how technical the descent was. There were arguments around TT vs Road bike, disc vs rim brakes, even carbon wheels vs aluminum. There was never really a choice for me, but it did add some uncertainty to what lay ahead. I had a very clear plan in my head to be conservative in my riding and to enjoy the fact I was achieving a long-held goal of riding up mountains in France. I had some self imposed limits also with regards to descent speed, again to stay within my abilities and ensure I made it safely onto the run course.

We had been told that the roads were closed but that there may be some local traffic out and about.

That was not the case. Sure, they closed off the right lane for the race, but there was a lot of traffic, particularly on the main climb as well as all of the villages we went through. Many were driving with little regard for the fact there was a race on and at the first aid station one was so eager to get through, he cause a rider in front of me to drop his bottle that went under my front wheel. I got lucky and was able avoid hitting the volunteers in front of me or the aid station table, but it was a close call.

The main climb was about 20km and was awesome. Some great views, some tunnels cut into the mountainside, rivers and more shade than I expected – but welcomed as it was still very hot. It was roughly the same as riding up Mt. Buffalo. I crossed paths with some Aussies and we had some brief chats on the way up, but for the most part everyone was silent as they knuckled down to the task at hand. Reaching the summit was a little anti-climatic as it didn’t offer anything spectacular and we were then into the next 60km section of rolling hills. Life got a little be tough at this point. I pinched a nerve or something in my neck and felt a bit crook when taking on the nutrition. I backed off on the power a little, got some nurofen into me and slowly but surely I started to come good, which I was really happy about because I wanted my wits about me for the descent.

Which. Was. Awesome!

The total descent was split into 2 sections with another 10km climb in between. At time of writing, I’ve been a bit disappointed in the photos that have come through – none are from the descent despite there being at least 1 camera set up that I was aware of. Sections of this descent have been used in movies like (one of the James Bond movies apparently) and I could see why. High elevation, rock tunnels, incredible views and switchbacks made for a great ride down. As long as you concentrated. There were some extremely steep drop-offs on some areas and you definitely didn’t want to go over the edge. 1 guy was probably lucky to hit the rock face rather than go over the edge. That option ended his day, the other would have ended his life.

There were some hero’s going too fast, they were the ones over-shooting the corners, there were a lot of really good descenders and then there were a lot like me – who rode within themselves and made sure they got off the mountain. The last section back into Nice was a lot of fun as the roads weren’t as steep and there were less hairpins. I was following someone who was riding at about the same ability as me so I sat (legally) in behind him and followed his line – was a lot of fun.

The last thing I want to say about the course is that I think it makes this event. It brings so much to the race and it requires a more complete cyclist to do well there. The downside was the amount of traffic as well as road ‘furniture’. Things like speed humps, sewer lids, water points, bollards etc meant that you had to be concentrating 100% of the time because there is less margin for error.

Starting the run I knew it would be tough. The sun was out, there was little to no shade and I wasn’t sure how my legs were after the ride. I felt ok on lap 1 (of 4) and made sure that at every opportunity I was tipping water on me, putting ice down my suit and trying to stay a cool as possible. I was feeling really comfortable with my pace and it was enough to go under 4hrs, which is always a goal at an IM.

On lap 2 my watch decided it had had enough and promptly flatted on me. This meant I had no idea of pace, cadence etc so I was running entirely on feel. I expected to slow significantly during the run and some mental calculations had me thinking I might be lucky and go under 13hrs.

Em was visible each lap and she knew I was tracking well for time, but had no way of really telling me, but easily understood my hand gesture for “my watch is flat”. She got the wakkatoo flag out at the right time, just before lap 4, just to give me that little push to go round once more. Not knowing times, and to be honest, not really caring about them I started thinking about what I’d be happy with at the finish line. I’d decided any time that started with a 12 would be awesome, but I’ll be happy with a 13-something. The last lap was a relief, but I also wanted to take it in – the crowds were big and lined pretty much the entire course.

Coming into the finishing chute was incredible and whilst I expected to be emotional, it was more a release of some very deep satisfaction in achieving a personal goal that meant a great deal to me, on a number of levels – I was just so damn happy with how I executed my race.

Crossing the line, I had nothing left in the tank so I’m surprised I was as animated as I was. I didn’t even look at my times, because I just didn’t care – I was just happy that I felt I’d put in a race that those who are invested in me doing these events would be proud of. To later see I not only went well under 12hours, but also ran a sub 4hr IM marathon for only the second time ever was an incredible surprise. Placing in the top 50% of my AG and the top 30% overall means I feel like I was able to hold my own and show I had the skills to race at this level.

To the entire Wakkatoo Crew- we bloody did it!

Some important thankyous:

First and foremost I need to thank my absolute #1 supporter Emmy. Her unwavering support during training, between races, when I’m on a high or struggling with motivation has been the primary reason I’ve been able to follow this sporting passion. Her efforts on race day to not only be in the right spot at the right time, with the right thing to say or do borders on supernatural. Balancing that with keeping everyone else informed as well as her own fatigue and they really should issue medals for Ironman support crew.

To my kids, and the rest of my wider family – thanks guys for the interest and support. You all know the motivation behind me doing these types of events and I appreciate the support. Kids- thanks for being the occasional training buddy, it helps more than you probably realize. Let’s do some more of that yeah?

There is a wider Wakkatoo Crew who tune into these races. It staggers me that so many people take an interest, but I am eternally grateful for it. You may not think it, but sometimes Em might pass on a message from you (or simply tell me you are following) and it’s great motivation.

And lastly a shout out to my coach Stephane from GPC Squad. Steph’s guidance and experience in the world of triathlon has been a major factor in me going from a weekend tri-hack to someone who can toe the start line of a world champs and feel like he belongs – thanks mate.
