Your first triathlon: how to start.

Start with your current fitness, swimming confidence and the time you can train. Choose a race and preparation timeline that suit your starting point.

You do not have to arrive as an experienced triathlete. This guide introduces the practical steps to consider before your first event.

Choose your race

Start with a beginner or sprint event. Typical distances:

RaceSwimBikeRun
Beginner300 to 500 m8 to 12 km2 to 3 km
Sprintabout 750 mabout 20 kmabout 5 km

Distances vary, so check the race you enter. Then book it. A date keeps you honest.

Gather the basics

  • Goggles, swimwear and a swim cap (the race usually supplies one).
  • A bike in good working order, and a helmet.
  • Running shoes and clothes you’re comfortable in.
  • A watch or phone app, if you want to record your sessions.

You may not need specialist triathlon equipment to start. Check the bike, helmet and wetsuit requirements for your chosen event.

Build up gradually

A simple week for most beginners:

  • 2 to 3 swims, short and regular. The swim is usually the biggest hurdle.
  • 2 to 3 rides, one a little longer.
  • 2 to 3 runs. Mix walking and running if you need to.
  • 1 rest day, and an easy day after your hardest.

Add a little time each week, not all at once. If something hurts, ease off and get it checked.

Practise the race

  • Brick session. Ride, then run straight away. Your legs will feel strange. Practising helps.
  • Open water. If your race has an open water swim, practise somewhere safe, with a friend, and check local conditions first.
  • Transition. Lay out your gear and run through the changeovers once at home.

Race week

Keep training short and easy. Check your bike. Pack the night before. Arrive early.

Pace the swim and the ride so you’ve got something left for the run.

Questions about your first triathlon

Do I need to be a good swimmer to do a triathlon?

No. You need to be able to swim the race distance at any pace, and you can rest on the wall or float if you need to. Many beginner races have short pool swims. Practise a few sessions in the pool before race day.

What is the best first triathlon distance?

A short one. Beginner and sprint triathlons are the usual starting points. A typical sprint is about a 750 m swim, 20 km ride and 5 km run, but distances vary, so check the race you pick.

How long does it take to train for a first triathlon?

There is no single timeline that suits everyone. Your starting fitness, swimming ability, event distance and available time all matter. Use the assessment to share where you are now.

What bike do I need?

Check the equipment rules for your chosen event. Your bike needs to be suitable for the course and in safe working order; the helmet requirements matter too.

Do I need a coach for my first triathlon?

No. Many people do their first race without one. A coach helps if you want a program built around your week and someone to check how it’s going.
